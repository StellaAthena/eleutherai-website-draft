Research/Datasets

What a model learns begins with what we choose to show it.

Datasets are not passive inputs. Their composition determines which capabilities are possible, which communities are represented, and which risks become embedded in a model. We build open datasets and study the decisions that shape them.

Public data should be inspectable infrastructure.

Open models are difficult to study when their training data cannot be examined or reproduced. From The Pile to the Common Pile, EleutherAI has built large-scale corpora that expose their sources, processing choices, and limitations to outside scrutiny.

Provenance is part of the dataset.

A useful corpus needs more than text. Researchers need to know where its contents came from, what permissions govern them, how they were transformed, and whose interests were excluded from those decisions.

Sources disappear

The public web is becoming less available for AI training, often through blunt restrictions that make it harder to distinguish consensual research from exploitative collection.

Consent in Crisis ↗

Records are incomplete

Across text, speech, and video datasets, missing or inconsistent source records prevent researchers from auditing legality, consent, representation, and downstream reuse.

The Data Provenance Initiative ↗

Documentation is research

Datasheets, catalogues, and data cards turn collection decisions into inspectable evidence instead of leaving them as undocumented implementation details.

Datasheet for The Pile ↗

Changing the data changes the model.

Filtering, duplication, contamination, and source mixture are interventions on learning. We study their effects directly so data decisions can become testable scientific and safety choices.

Capability

Domain-specific corpora such as OpenWebMath and BigBIO make it possible to build and evaluate models around forms of expertise neglected by generic web data.

Measurement

Test contamination can inflate evaluation results and obscure generalization. Its effects depend on dose, task, and where contaminated examples enter the training process.

Safety

Selective pretraining-data filtering can prevent the acquisition of dangerous knowledge and produce safeguards that survive later tampering and fine-tuning.

Deep Ignorance demonstrates that filtering pretraining data can build tamper-resistant safeguards into open-weight models.Best Paper Runner-up, NeurIPS Biosecurity Workshop
