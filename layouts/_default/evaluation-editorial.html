Research/Evaluation

A score is not a measurement.

Evaluation shapes what the field recognizes as progress. We build open systems for producing results, then study when those results are reproducible, comparable, and capable of supporting the claims made from them.

The benchmark is part of the experiment.

Language-model evaluations are often presented as fixed rulers: run the model, record the score, compare it with another model. But the score depends on task construction, prompts, model interfaces, aggregation, and the benchmark's changing relationship to the systems it measures.

EleutherAI's evaluation research treats that full chain as an object of scientific study. Open infrastructure makes the experiment inspectable. Empirical work tests whether the instrument still discriminates. Reporting systems preserve the context needed to interpret the result.

Benchmarks lose resolution.

As models improve, familiar benchmarks can collapse toward a ceiling. Differences remain visible as decimal points even after they stop being statistically meaningful.

Evaluation Cards ↗

Evidence should travel with the score.

Evaluation Cards and Every Eval Ever recover methodological context from results scattered across papers, leaderboards, and model cards. The goal is not another ranking. It is a public evidence layer where results can be traced, compared, and challenged.

EleutherAI develops this work with the EvalEval Coalition, which EleutherAI sponsors. Stella Biderman serves on its steering committee.

What we are trying to make possible

Independent researchers should be able to reproduce evaluation claims, identify when an instrument has failed, and examine what existing reports leave out. That requires open implementations, longitudinal evidence, and reporting structures designed for scrutiny rather than a single headline number.
